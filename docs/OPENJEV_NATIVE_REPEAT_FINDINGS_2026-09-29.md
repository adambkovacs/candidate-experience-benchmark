# OpenJev native repeat: three P0 modes, three fresh passes each

All three fresh fixed-mode passes completed 60 development reviews with valid outputs. Each matched all four provisional reference fields on **53/60** reviews: sentiment 56/60, follow-up 60/60, serious concern 57/60 and testimonial potential 60/60. Every pair had zero flips across 60 shared valid reviews, and no four-field prediction changed across the three passes. The all-field range is 53–53/60. Identical answers on this fixed set do not prove deterministic behavior on other inputs or runtimes.

The same seven reviews missed at least one reference field in each pass. Three mixed-sentiment references were classified as negative; one insufficient-information sentiment reference was classified as neutral. Three insufficient-information serious-concern references were classified as no. Follow-up and testimonial decisions matched the provisional references for all 60 reviews in every pass. Those references are provisional AI-reviewed labels, not independent human ground truth.

All three fresh adaptive-mode passes also completed 60 valid reviews each. Each matched all four reference fields on **52/60**: sentiment 54/60, follow-up 60/60, serious concern 57/60 and testimonial potential 60/60. Every adaptive pair had zero flips across 60 shared valid reviews, and its all-field range is 52–52/60. Four mixed-sentiment references were classified as negative, one neutral reference as negative, and one insufficient-information reference as neutral. Three insufficient-information serious-concern references were classified as no.

For each corresponding pass, fixed and adaptive differed on the same four sentiment decisions: DEV-019, DEV-020 and DEV-030 agreed with the reference only in fixed mode, while DEV-059 agreed only in adaptive mode. The 53/60 versus 52/60 all-field gap is a descriptive comparison between different controls on these records. Zero within-mode repeat flips do not isolate which part of adaptive execution caused a cross-mode difference.

All three thinking-mode passes completed 60 valid development reviews. They matched all four reference fields on **53/60, 54/60 and 54/60**, a range of 53–54/60. Sentiment agreement was 57/60 in each pass; follow-up was 57/60, 58/60 and 58/60; serious concern was 58/60, 57/60 and 58/60; testimonial potential was 60/60 throughout. Pairwise comparisons changed at least one answer on 9/60 reviews between fresh1 and fresh2, 9/60 between fresh1 and fresh3, and 4/60 between fresh2 and fresh3. Eleven distinct reviews changed across the three passes. Sentiment changed on four reviews across the triplet, follow-up on four and serious concern on four; no testimonial decision changed. These counts use all 60 shared valid reviews per pair.

In thinking fresh3, all eight provisional mixed-sentiment cases and all 25 reference-positive serious-concern cases were classified as such. Its three sentiment errors were one insufficient-information case marked neutral, one negative case marked neutral and one neutral case marked negative. It made two follow-up errors in opposite directions, and marked two insufficient-information serious-concern cases as no. These are errors against provisional AI-reviewed labels, not independently adjudicated ground truth. The same 57/60 sentiment score in each thinking pass masks changed decisions.

Thinking fresh1 predictions differ from fixed fresh1 on 12 reviews and adaptive fresh1 on 12 reviews; in each comparison, seven sentiment, three follow-up and three serious-concern decisions differ. Equal 53/60 scores for thinking fresh1 and fixed do not mean identical predictions. These are descriptive comparisons of distinct modes, not evidence that thinking caused a score change.
