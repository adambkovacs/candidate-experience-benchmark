# SemIf generated repeat: first P0/P1/P2 pass closed

The [fresh generated plan](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/manifest.json) schedules three P0/P1/P2 passes, nine 60-review development phases in total. Its first [P0](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P0/development.completion.json), [P1](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P1/development.completion.json) and [P2](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P2/development.completion.json) phases are closed in this [source-bound report](../public-site/semif-generated-repeats.json). Each saved 60 responses. All-four-field agreement against provisional references was **35/60 for P0, 26/60 for P1 and 43/60 for P2**. Later phases have no score in this publication snapshot. No prompt condition has a repeat pass yet, so repeat stability is unmeasured.

| Fresh1 measure | P0 out of 60 | P1 out of 60 | P2 out of 60 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| Sentiment agrees | 46 | 33 | 51 |

P0 had eight invalid responses, P1 had 22 and P2 had two. Every invalid response ended with `stop` and returned the same JSON schema text instead of four classification values. The [P0](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P0/development.raw.jsonl), [P1](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P1/development.raw.jsonl) and [P2](../results/repeatability-v1/semif-generated-fresh-v1/fresh1/P2/development.raw.jsonl) raw captures and parsed records in the feed retain those outcomes. P0's invalid IDs were DEV-002, DEV-020, DEV-029, DEV-040, DEV-044, DEV-051, DEV-055 and DEV-060; P2's were DEV-029 and DEV-055. The feed lists all 22 P1 IDs. Invalid positions remain in every fixed 60-review score denominator. Validity describes response format; agreement describes a match to the reference.

P1 scored nine fewer fixed-denominator all-field matches than P0. Fifteen P0-valid reviews became invalid in P1; only DEV-002 moved from invalid to valid. Among the **37 reviews valid in both**, two changed a four-field answer, DEV-013 and DEV-024, but shared-valid all-field agreement stayed **25/37 in both**. The fixed-60 score drop therefore reflects changed output validity as well as the common-set classifications; it is not a nine-review decline on a constant valid set.

P2 scored eight more fixed-denominator all-field matches than P0. Six P0-invalid reviews became valid in P2, and none moved from valid to invalid. Among the **52 reviews valid in both**, six changed at least one classification: DEV-001, DEV-006, DEV-013, DEV-018, DEV-049 and DEV-054. All-field agreement on that shared-valid set rose from **35/52 to 39/52**. Four of the six newly valid reviews also matched all four reference fields in P2. Against P1, P2 scored 17 more fixed-denominator matches; among the 38 reviews valid in both, six changed answers and shared-valid agreement rose from **26/38 to 29/38**. Another 20 P1-invalid reviews became valid in P2. These score differences combine validity turnover and changed classifications. They do not isolate an effect of the added instructions.

The 60 P0 development requests recorded **616.33 seconds** in summed client-observed elapsed time and returned **96,930 input** and **4,680 output tokens**. P1 recorded **742.83 seconds**, **107,550 input** and **8,070 output tokens**; P2 recorded **506.19 seconds**, **162,930 input** and **3,204 output tokens**. These sums are not isolated model inference time, and local hardware and electricity cost were not measured. The frozen plan specifies local MLX, temperature zero, thinking disabled, one attempt per review, and a fresh process and model load for each stage. It binds the intended requests without sending reference labels to the model.
