What a decision model is

A decision model takes text and a typed question, and returns one of your options with a probability in one pass.

System 1: the quick gut call

It works when the answer is one of a short list and each question asks about one thing. It can't invent a label.

Is a harassment report a serious concern? Jev caught all 25.

System 2: stopping to ask if it's the right question

It takes your words literally and can't say why it answered. It won't tell you when the question doesn't fit.

"Train got cancelled... Really helpful of them." Is that a testimonial?

Sourcedocs/talk/02-landscape.md Part A (TypeSafe docs: introduction, concepts/system-one, model-jaggedness/jev-1.13), findings.json referenceDistributions, DEV-027 in disputed-reviews-v1.json

So, a decision model. You send it text and a typed question with your options, and you get back one of those options and a probability, in one pass. It can't invent a label and it can't explain itself. That's System One. The "wait, is this even the right question?" check is System Two, and you build that yourself.

Typed output fixes the format, and the decision can still be wrong.

    The rule: the policy and the queue

    The rule says what a model may auto-accept. The escalation lines say who reads the rest.

    1. Run two decision models on every review.
    2. Auto-accept only when all four answers are identical.
    3. If either model flags a serious concern, a person reads it.
    4. If either model says can't tell, a person reads it.
    5. Keep the confidence score for the record, and don't use it to decide.
