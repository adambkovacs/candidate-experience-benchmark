    The rule. Agree or defer. Two cheap models on every review, accept only identical answers, send the rest to a person.

    I fixed the rule before computing any pair, then looked at all 21 pairs of the seven decision models. Five pairs let zero errors through. [S12 sorts] Solar Decide plus Perplexity Decider agreed on 53 and sent 7 to a person, for under four cents for both, and I picked it after seeing all 21, so it's an example.     Zero errors on these 60 is not zero errors on the next 60. And agreement only helps when the models fail differently: six of the 21 pairs accepted the soup with the same wrong answer. A second frontier model is a weak check for the same reason: seven models from three vendors gave one identical answer set, differing from our key only on the three disputed labels.

    The rule · the policy and the queue

    1. Two typed models on every review.
    2. Accept only identical four-field answers.
    3. Serious concern from either model: a person.
    4. Can't tell from either model: a person.
    5. Log confidence; don't gate on it.

    Q: Isn't your key the weak point? A: Partly, yes. It is v0.2, checked by people, and a separate AI review disputes three labels. Flipping one of them moves 212 runs up and 168 down. When a one-point difference decides a vendor, your label quality is the bottleneck.

    Q: Your key was written by an OpenAI model. Aren't you measuring who thinks like GPT? A: Possibly in part. People checked all 60, and if GPT-style models had a home advantage, OpenAI's own decision model didn't get it: Luna Decisions matched 49. I can't rule out that LLM-written text suits LLMs, which is one reason the key is provisional.

    Q: Sixty synthetic reviews. How real is that? A: Not real-world. An OpenAI assistant wrote the 60 reviews and drafted the key, people checked all 60 on 2 October, and the 340 planned validation records were never generated. Nothing here estimates performance, prevalence or fairness on real candidate text.

    Limits, and the claims we do not make.

    • Not a leaderboard. Runs differ in route, batch size, effort and prompt implementation.
    • Not causal. Prompt and effort comparisons describe saved runs.
    • Not real-world accuracy. 60 synthetic reviews; the 340 planned validation records were never generated.
    • The key is provisional. v0.2, drafted by an AI assistant, checked by people, three labels disputed.
    • Missing cost is unknown, not zero.
    • No speed ranking. Client time mixes network, CLI, batch and operator time.
    • No pooling of the 1,004 run entries into one score.

    Q: So which model is best? A: I can't rank them. Runs differ in route, batch size, effort and prompt implementation. Several setups reached 59, including a 26B open-weight model, and that describes one pass each.
